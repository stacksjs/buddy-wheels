# Buddy Rover r0 bench assembly

Configuration rover-r0-l1300-w700-h1000-r1000-a560-g1000-hub101.6-trail-swivel310-k350. All dimensions mm. BENCH DESIGN. NOT FOR ANIMAL USE. No payload, speed, distance, slope or surface rating exists. Buddy is owner-reported 140 lb; his measurements and suitability for a carrier are not established.

## What this revision is

A large cut-to-fit carrier built from 1 inch square aluminum tube, 1/8 inch wall 6061-T6 for the chassis and 1/16 inch wall 6063-T52 for the cabin, three repeated flat metal profiles, plywood panels and printed service parts. The tailgate is the ramp: it folds down to a 8.0 degree walk-in with no step, and stands on its own two feet so the dog's weight on the threshold goes to the ground instead of tipping the carrier. Two 15 inch trail (4.00-8) wheels at the rear and a 10 inch swivel caster on a raised nose at the front, so it steers by pushing the handle sideways.

Coordinates: X forward, Y across, Z up. Origin on the ground under the rear axle.

## Planning statics, not test results

With a 72.6 kg (160 lb) planning mass centered in the cabin, its center sits 90 mm ahead of the rear axle and the front wheel carries about 10% of that mass. The swivel caster steers without lifting anything. Lifting the front to climb a curb takes roughly 113 N (25 lbf) down on the handle. A swivel caster can shimmy at a run, so lock the swivel with its bolt-on lock before running and steer by lifting the front as with a fixed wheel. A dog that stands at the tailgate moves the center behind the axle. The two tip-stop feet exist for that case and their setting and strength are unproven.

## Joint patterns

Every joint is a flat gusset with 1/4 inch bolts through steel crush sleeves. Hole centers are measured from the joint centerline, where two tube centerlines cross.

| Profile | Qty | Holes from the joint centerline | Where |

| Corner gusset | 44 | Leg A 50 and 80. Leg B 35 and 65 | Corner posts on the outside face only, axle-post tops both faces, rail to cross member, roof corners, handle grip, ramp legs |
| Tee gusset | 8 | Bar 30 each side. Stem 35 and 60 | Intermediate base cross members, mid roof bow, fork arms top and bottom |
| Axle hanger, 15 inch trail (4.00-8) wheel | 4 | Bar 30 each side. Stem 105, 135. Axle bore at 68.9 | Both faces of each axle post and each fork stub |

Legs A and B alternate where two gussets meet on one tube, so bolts cross 15 mm apart and never share a station. Tube bores are 9.725 for the sleeves, plate holes 6.75, axle sleeve bores 19.25. All are nominal. Use the three printed drill guides for pilot centers and finish with metal tooling.

## Build order

1. Print the clearance coupon and measure it. Print one cap and one clip before batching.
2. Cut and deburr every tube. Mark each with its ID.
3. Base frame: two side rails, 5 cross members. Corner gussets under each corner, tee gussets under each intermediate member.
4. Axle posts at X = 0 with paired hangers. The hanger bar bolts to the rail, the stem to the post. Fit the 19.05 axle sleeve through post and both hangers.
5. Corner posts with one corner gusset on the outside face, leg A up the post, and washers on the inside.
6. Top rails, three top cross tubes, corner gussets on top, tee gussets at the mid bow.
7. Nose: the base rails run 327.85 past the front cross member. Stand four nose posts on them at X = 927.55 and 1013.25, each with a pair of corner gussets, the rail leg pointing away from the other post. Lay the two caster beams across the post tops and tie each to its posts with one corner gusset on the outside face. Hold the caster plate up under the beams at the centerline, mark its four holes, drill, and bolt it with sleeves.
8. Floor panel on bedding tape, fastened to the frame.
9. Ramp: panel, two curb rails on top, two ribs underneath at 40 mm and mid length, stand legs on the hinge-end rib 60 mm in from each edge. Hinge leaves on the top faces of floor and ramp.
10. Handle arms on the outside of the rear and axle posts at Z = 1000, one 3 inch bolt per post. Grip tube between the arm ends.
11. Wheels, washers and cotters. Rear stack between outer washer faces 149.05, with 0.5 total end float.
12. Tip-stop feet, the two brake arms with their plates and clamps, latches, safety strap. Then the cover.

## The ramp

Lowered, the panel runs from the deck at 151.9 down to the ground over 1000. The two capped stand legs under the hinge end touch the ground directly below the threshold, so the hinge carries no bending on level ground. The walking width between the curb rails is 637.2. Three printed toes slide onto the ground edge and take the last 20 mm down to nothing.

Raised, the ramp is the tailgate. Two draw latches hold it to the rear posts and the safety strap is a second, independent hold. The stand legs then point backward about 110 mm at shin height. The handle reaches 380 mm behind the tailgate. Stride clearance has to be checked by a runner before this layout is accepted.

Each rear axle end stands 14.525 mm into the cabin, about 41 mm above the deck. Cover it and review it for fur, paws and hips.

Meshes are exported in print orientation at 1:1 in millimeters and fit a 220 x 220 x 100 mm envelope. Never scale one to fit. Use PETG for indoor fit trials and ASA for parts that will live in sun. Starting point only: 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers, four walls, 30% infill; five walls and 50% for the ramp toes, which carry paw load against the ground. Print clips on end so the snap flexes along the layers. The ramp toe slot is an 18 mm overhang and needs support or a slower bridge. Record filament, settings, measured fit, grams and failures.

Files prefixed FIT_CHECK_ONLY are plastic copies of metal parts for checking hole patterns. Cut the real ones from metal/*.dxf. No printed part here carries the dog, a wheel, the ramp hinge, a latch, a brake or a tether.

## Release gates

1. Buddy's measurements, lying length and standing height first, and a veterinary view on whether riding suits him.
2. Engineering review of the frame, the axle hangers, the ramp and its stand, the handle and the tip-stops, including steel-to-aluminum corrosion.
3. A measured wheel: actual hub length, bearing fit and a rating that suits the carrier, then the parking brake proven on a slope with ballast. The 13 inch candidate is a garden-cart wheel for walking pace; a running wheel is still an open selection. Until wheel and brake are proven the running gear is a placeholder. There is no brake for slowing while moving.
4. Ballast tests to an approved plan: static proof, ramp load at mid span and at the threshold, tip in every direction, curb and brake events, fatigue.
5. Cover tests listed in materials.md.
6. Only then a supervised, consented fit and handling study with stop rules.

Digital mesh checks and a tidy drawing prove none of this.

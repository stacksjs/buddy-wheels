# Buddy Rover r0 bench assembly

Configuration rover-r0-l1300-w700-h1000-r1000-a560-g1000-hub101.6-trail-swivel310-k350-t180-welded-sprung. All dimensions mm. BENCH DESIGN. NOT FOR ANIMAL USE. No payload, speed, distance, slope or surface rating exists. Buddy is owner-reported 140 lb; his measurements and suitability for a carrier are not established.

## What this revision is

A large cut-to-fit carrier built from 1 inch square aluminum tube, 1/8 inch wall 6061-T6 for the chassis and 1/16 inch wall 6063-T52 for the cabin, three repeated flat metal profiles, plywood panels and printed service parts. The tailgate is the ramp: it folds down to a 8.0 degree walk-in with no step, and stands on its own two feet so the dog's weight on the threshold goes to the ground instead of tipping the carrier. Two 15 inch trail (4.00-8) wheels at the rear and a 10 inch swivel caster on a raised nose at the front, so it steers by pushing the handle sideways.

Coordinates: X forward, Y across, Z up. Origin on the ground under the rear axle.

## Planning statics, not test results

With a 72.6 kg (160 lb) planning mass centered in the cabin, its center sits 90 mm ahead of the rear axle and the front wheel carries about 10% of that mass. The swivel caster steers without lifting anything. Lifting the front to climb a curb takes roughly 108 N (24 lbf) down on the handle. A swivel caster can shimmy at a run, so lock the swivel with its bolt-on lock before running and steer by lifting the front as with a fixed wheel. A dog that stands at the tailgate moves the center behind the axle. The two tip-stop feet exist for that case and their setting and strength are unproven.

## Joints

The frame is welded by a fabrication shop and arrives in one piece: 29 tubes, 10 bends and 36 welded joints, with no gussets. The base, posts and cross tubes are square tube. The bent tubes are round, 1 inch across with a 1/8 inch wall, so a mail-order service can cut and bend them. Each cabin side is one hoop bent from a single tube: up the rear, along the roof, which falls 180 from rear to front, 10.2 degrees, and down the front pillar, which leans back 22.7 degrees from upright. The handle is one bent bar and the caster hangs from two bent hoops across the nose.

## Build order

1. Print the clearance coupon and measure it. Print one cap and one clip before batching.
2. The shop cuts and bends the tubes from cut-list.csv and bend-schedule.csv. The axle posts and the front foot of each side hoop have an angled end.
3. The shop tacks the base ladder flat: two side rails, 5 cross members. Check the diagonals, then weld.
4. Axle posts at X = 0, then a side hoop on each rail, resting on its axle post, then the two nose hoops. Tack, check both axle posts are square and in line, then weld.
5. Three top cross tubes between the roof runs of the hoops. Handle bar on the outside of the rear and axle posts at Z = 1000. Brake arms on the axle posts.
6. Drill for the hangers, the brake plates, the caster and the tip-stops after welding, from drilling.csv.
7. Swing arms on their pivot bolts outside the base rails, shocks between the tabs, axle sleeves through the arms. Bolt the caster under the beams; its swivel axis sits 205 ahead of the cabin front.
8. Floor panel on bedding tape, fastened to the frame.
9. Ramp: panel, two curb rails on top, two ribs underneath at 40 mm and mid length, stand legs welded to the hinge-end rib 60 mm in from each edge. Hinge leaves on the top faces of floor and ramp.
10. Wheels, washers and cotters. Rear stack between outer washer faces 142.7, with 0.5 total end float.
11. Tip-stop feet, the brake plates and clamps, latches, safety strap. Then the cover.

## The ramp

Lowered, the panel runs from the deck at 151.9 down to the ground over 1000. The two capped stand legs under the hinge end touch the ground directly below the threshold, so the hinge carries no bending on level ground. The walking width between the curb rails is 637.2. Three printed toes slide onto the ground edge and take the last 20 mm down to nothing.

Raised, the ramp is the tailgate. Two draw latches hold it to the rear posts and the safety strap is a second, independent hold. The stand legs then point backward about 110 mm at shin height. The handle reaches 380 mm behind the tailgate. Stride clearance has to be checked by a runner before this layout is accepted.

The rear axles sit in the swing arms outside the frame, so nothing stands into the cabin at the axle. The carrier is 1078.9 wide over the axle ends.

## Rear suspension

Each rear wheel hangs on a trailing swing arm outside the base rail. The arm pivots on one 1/2 inch bolt through the rail, 300 mm ahead of the axle, and a coil-over shock runs from a pair of plates bolted to the arm up to a pair hung from the axle post around a short spacer tube. Nothing in it is welded, so it fits either frame.

Meshes are exported in print orientation at 1:1 in millimeters and fit a 220 x 220 x 100 mm envelope. Never scale one to fit. Use PETG for indoor fit trials and ASA for parts that will live in sun. Starting point only: 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers, four walls, 30% infill; five walls and 50% for the ramp toes, which carry paw load against the ground. Print clips on end so the snap flexes along the layers. The ramp toe slot is an 18 mm overhang and needs support or a slower bridge. Record filament, settings, measured fit, grams and failures.

Files prefixed FIT_CHECK_ONLY are plastic copies of metal parts for checking hole patterns. Cut the real ones from metal/*.dxf. No printed part here carries the dog, a wheel, the ramp hinge, a latch, a brake or a tether.

## Release gates

1. Buddy's measurements, lying length and standing height first, and a veterinary view on whether riding suits him.
2. Engineering review of the frame, the axle hangers, the ramp and its stand, the handle and the tip-stops, including steel-to-aluminum corrosion.
3. A measured wheel: actual hub length, bearing fit and a rating that suits the carrier, then the parking brake proven on a slope with ballast. The 13 inch candidate is a garden-cart wheel for walking pace; a running wheel is still an open selection. Until wheel and brake are proven the running gear is a placeholder. There is no brake for slowing while moving.
4. Ballast tests to an approved plan: static proof, ramp load at mid span and at the threshold, tip in every direction, curb and brake events, fatigue.
5. Cover tests listed in materials.md.
6. Only then a supervised, consented fit and handling study with stop rules.

Digital mesh checks and a tidy drawing prove none of this.

## Included design

A rear woven support saddle with two rounded leg openings, an open rear hygiene channel, removable foam and spacer-mesh lining; a padded front bib; chest and shoulder webbing; four rear suspension tails, two front tethers, reinforcing bands, closed frame wraps, protective chafe sleeves, metal adjuster envelopes and a front-only closure. Body load travels through the woven shell and reinforced webbing into metal frame members. The mesh is lining, not the sole support. Caps and printed fit-check parts carry no harness load.

## Candidate sewing sequence

1. Make paper and non-load mockups first. Record Buddy's supported groin height, actual chest girth, each upper-thigh circumference, hind-leg spacing and body length. Confirm anatomy, elimination clearance and skin sensitivity with the fitting professional. The starting settings are bench estimates.
2. Cut SG01-SG06 from the exact selected patterns and mark fore/aft, left/right and warp direction. Rear opening faces positive X. Keep the rear hygiene channel open. Inspect that seams or binding do not close a leg opening or migrate toward sensitive tissue.
3. Lay the rear reinforcing webbing under the woven shell, along the two side edges and front edge, with two separate rear cross pieces that stop 3 mm before each side of the hygiene channel. Retain 60 mm candidate sewing laps at their reinforced joins, directed along the webbing route rather than across a cut opening. Four suspension tails meet those reinforcements at the four corner zones. No tail may terminate on mesh or foam alone. Mark each overlap and keep its stitching clear of the leg openings.
4. Candidate bench stitch plan: bonded polyester Tex 90 thread, lockstitch, nominal 3 mm stitch length. Two independent stitch rows 4 and 8 mm from a bound cut edge; webbing overlaps use a 60 mm rectangular box with two diagonals and a second perimeter row. This is a reproducible coupon proposal, NOT a validated strength specification. Qualify exact material stacks, needle/thread/tension, machine settings, seam efficiency, tear-out and fatigue before approving production. If the sewing setup damages fibers, stop and redesign it.
5. Fit the rear pad and mesh over the woven support. Keep pad/mesh removable for inspection and cleaning using a bound cover opening and non-load hook-and-loop closure. Its closure must never substitute for structural stitching. Check seam bulk and bare-edge contact on a mock body, not Buddy.
6. Assemble the front bib layers with bound edges. Add chest and shoulder bands to the woven reinforcement. The front buckle closes restraint webbing only. Keep it outside rear body-support paths. Shoulder/chest routing is a layout candidate; the throat, axillae and foreleg motion must remain clear in the approved fit.
7. Sew the 12 chafe-sleeve panels into six sleeves with 10 mm seams, then fit them onto the frame wraps. Each wrap has a centerline perimeter plus two 60 mm return tails sewn to its reinforcement tab. Qualify those load seams as a stack, not a guessed simple end overlap. Rear wraps enclose the rear metal bridge/bracket stack; front support wraps enclose the side rails. Two L-shaped keeper runs, with reinforced corner joins, follow the outer side rails and then the rear bridge to tie their wraps together, so the system remains captured to the metal frame rather than a printed end cap. Keep these runs along the metal members, never diagonally through body space. Front-harness tethers have separate rail wraps. Assemble wraps before closing the frame if required. Do not force a loop over sharp metal, an adjuster or a pin head.
8. The studio shows nominal bracket/loop envelopes. Actual rail/bracket corners, fasteners, sleeve thickness and loop perimeter must be checked at every size. Webbing migration, edge loading and keeper extension require loaded fixture tests. These wraps are NOT approved anchors merely because they are drawn around metal. Do not drill extra frame holes or substitute hose clamps, printed buckles or snap clips without a reviewed redesign.
9. Thread metal adjusters using their actual manufacturer routing, with a captive sewn return and retained free tail. No approved adjuster SKU exists yet, so do not assume the pictured two-bar envelope is purchasable or slip-resistant. The model shows unthreaded placement below the tape; actual over/under routing follows the approved hardware drawing. In a guarded empty fixture, measure adjuster slip, tail pull-through, seam failure and anchor migration under an approved load protocol before any animal. Unload completely before adjusting/removing a support.
10. Qualify dry/wet/dirty/salt-exposed material stacks, abrasion over the actual metal finish, washing/drying, sweat/urine compatibility, sustained creep, cyclic loading, asymmetric entry and redundant capture. Measure loaded sag, pressure distribution, stride and stability using an approved clinical/engineering protocol. A raw-webbing breaking number or mesh upholstery rating is not a wheelchair/harness rating.

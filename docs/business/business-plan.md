# Buddy Wheels business plan

Version 0.1, November 7, 2026. Prepared for Chris Breuer. Assumptions are proposals unless explicitly sourced. The complete product is in development and accepts no money today.

## 1. Executive decision

Build an independent, repairable rear-support dog wheelchair line using printable fitting parts and standard mechanical components. Start with one size family, establish a repeatable fitting and manufacturing process, then qualify smaller and larger families separately. The planned range is XS through XL, covering a proposed 2-180 lb envelope. This is an intended development range, not a load rating.

The immediate reason is Buddy's loss of hind-leg use. Buddy is very large, and the founder found few available options in a suitable size. Large-dog availability and fit are central to the product, alongside affordability. This is the founder's experience, not a verified market-wide availability claim. The business must not become a reason to postpone his veterinary care or make him the first uncontrolled structural test. Pursue an available professionally fitted aid for Buddy while the product is developed.

The price objective is approximately half of the reference product. Observed reference prices support targets of $159.50 for S, $224.50 for M and $264.50 for L/XL. XS has an independent $129 target because a matching reference was not verified. These are pre-tax, pre-delivery product targets, not current sale prices. If qualified production cannot support them, change the design or the offer transparently; do not reduce validation to meet a price.

The proposed initial offer is a complete kit with fit review, adjustment instructions, replaceable parts and a documented service path. Later offers can include maker kits, replacement components and a refurbishing program. Offer digital production files only after the associated process and safety boundaries are understood. The website already distinguishes shopping preview from an available product and blocks order placement at the server.

## 2. Customer problem and audience

The core customer is a caregiver whose dog has been assessed by a veterinarian and may benefit from mobility support. Their urgent questions are whether the aid suits their dog, how to fit it, what happens if it does not fit, how quickly it can arrive, and what it costs in total. A low advertised price without service, safety and availability is insufficient.

The second audience is rehabilitation practices and rescues that fit several dogs and understand where a rear cart is inappropriate. They can help validate fit, identify recurrent failure modes and operate loaner or refurbishment programs. These are potential partners, not existing relationships. Do not imply their endorsement.

The problem is large and frequent for an affected household, but purchase frequency is low. This is durable hardware, not subscription software. The business should earn enough on an initial sale and responsible service; it must not depend on imagined recurring subscription revenue. Replacement parts should be affordable and safe to replace, not deliberately fragile profit centers.

Proposed first segment: caregivers within one reachable US service region, with professional fitting access, seeking professionally appropriate rear support, with large and giant dogs central to initial discovery. Exclude complex full-support cases from the first product. The first family remains unselected until Buddy's measurements and professional feedback are available. Prioritize the large-dog fit gap rather than assuming medium dogs should launch first. The shop initially displays L as a product concept, not a size recommendation.

## 3. Product and differentiation

The November 8 portfolio expansion adds Buddy Rover, an independent adventure-carrier design focused on long-distance running, beach walks and winter conversion. Its Run, Coast, Snow and All-season configuration briefs are priced separately from the wheelchair families. See the [Rover product plan](../product/buddy-rover.md) and [unit economics](rover-unit-economics.md). Existing wheelchair demand scenarios and startup budget do not include Rover. Release the first qualified family/configuration from evidence rather than trying to launch the entire portfolio at once.

The design thesis is modularity. Use standard metal tubes as the primary rails, replaceable wheel/axle assemblies, washable soft support, and printable junctions, adjustment parts and non-structural covers. Separate the geometries used to fit the dog from the parts that provide a dependable load path. Printable does not mean entirely plastic.

Proposed advantages to test are lower complete-kit price, simpler part replacement, adjustable width/length/height, clear measurement guidance, and a maker-friendly repair system. None is yet proven superior to the reference. Validate them through assembly-time observations, controlled repair trials, fatigue testing, fit outcomes and total-cost comparisons.

Five size families are a portfolio roadmap. XS needs very low mass and a low center of gravity. Short-legged dogs need geometry independent of body weight. Large and giant families are first-priority candidates for a focused development slice, subject to Buddy's measurements and professional review. L and XL require separate load, tip stability and transport work. Growing dogs, deep chests, long backs, unusually wide bodies and front-limb impairment need manual review rather than an automatic fit promise.

The intended complete kit includes the qualified frame modules, standard wheels and axles, hardware, soft support, fitting instructions and an inspection checklist. Final contents and adjustment limits remain open. Do not advertise an all-terrain capability, folding mechanism, front conversion or compatibility with another brand until implemented and tested.

Active outdoor use is a development priority. Investigate adjustable wheel suspension and tire cushioning against a fixed-frame baseline. Treat controlled body-weight support for professionally guided rehabilitation as a separate research track, with calibration and independent safeguards. Neither is included in today's target kit price. The [suspension research plan](../product/suspension-research.md) defines evidence, cost and release gates before committing to an optional module or advanced family.

## 4. Market discovery and sizing

No verified total market size is available. Use bottom-up discovery rather than multiplying the global dog population by an invented disability rate. Conduct 20 caregiver interviews and 8 professional interviews before setting a volume plan. Ask about their actual purchase experience, fit attempts, total spend, time to support, returns and repair needs. Avoid asking only whether they like a cheap wheelchair.

Triangulate demand through three channels: potential fitting partners' historical eligible cases, qualified website inquiries once a real consent-backed form exists, and public competitor availability/price observations. Partner case counts must be anonymized and explicitly permitted. Do not scrape health records.

A testable initial serviceable-market worksheet is: reachable practices × eligible annual cases per practice × percentage reachable through a pilot × conversion after fitting assessment. Every factor needs a range and an evidence source. The obtainable market is additionally capped by qualified production capacity and fit-support hours. The model deliberately does not publish a TAM dollar headline today.

A 200-kit first commercial-year case is a planning scenario, not a forecast. It is below the capacity of a large factory and above a casual maker project. Validate printer hours, support load and referral demand before committing to it. Set stop conditions if suitable demand is too small or unit contribution becomes negative.

## 5. Competitive position

The linked Walkin' Pets line is the initial price and feature reference. It has an offered product and an established fit/support proposition. Buddy Wheels has neither a proven performance record nor existing customer outcomes. Price comparison alone does not establish an interchangeable substitute.

The position is: repairable mobility hardware for caregivers who want clear fit guidance and an affordable complete kit. Keep the tone practical, patient and hopeful. Show what is printed, what is standard, how a part is replaced and what evidence exists. Avoid claims that competitors overcharge or that 3D printing inherently improves safety.

Before commercialization, compare at least three current alternatives using dated total delivered prices, included support, intended weight/geometry range, adjustment, repair supply, guarantee and returns. Preserve quotations and product links. Complete brand/domain checks and independent intellectual-property review. The working name Buddy Wheels and GitHub repository do not establish trademark rights.

## 6. Revenue and offer architecture

Start with the complete kit. It is easier to control manufacturing, inspect fit hardware, provide known support components and trace failures when one party assembles and releases the product. A lower-priced box of parts can hide customer labor and fitting cost, so quote the complete delivered offer before collecting payment.

Proposed later offers:

| Offer | Value | Gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Complete kit | Qualified hardware, support and documented assembly | Product, fitting and fulfillment approval |
| Maker kit | Standard hardware plus approved printed parts or qualified print specification | Reproducible process and supported maker qualification |
| Replacement modules | Repairs without replacing the entire chair | Clear compatibility and service instructions |
| Refurbished/loaner kits | Lower entry cost and reduced waste | Inspection, sanitation and life-limit policy |
| Digital files | Local manufacturing and educational inspection | Licensing, version control and explicit process qualification |

No subscription is required for a dog to keep using its chair. If professional fitting is separately charged, disclose that prominently. Do not subsidize the advertised price with undisclosed required accessories. A sponsorship fund could support rescue placements after commercial economics are demonstrated; it is not included as committed funding.

## 7. Unit economics and price discipline

Use integer cents throughout the app and financial model. `bun run finance` generates the current scenario tables from versioned assumptions. The current complete-kit COGS assumptions are $58 XS, $72 S, $99 M, $125 L and $155 XL. They include material, purchased parts, print capacity, assembly, inspection and packaging allocations, but require supplier quotes and measured labor.

Contribution subtracts COGS, outbound delivery subsidy, assumed payment cost, warranty/return reserve, acquisition cost and variable support. A 2.9% plus $0.30 payment cost is only a scenario input; it is not a verified processor quote. A 5% revenue reserve is an accounting assumption, not evidence that returns will stay below 5%.

The XL price target creates the narrowest cushion. Heavy wheels, stronger rails, larger packaging, extra support and freight can make it uneconomic at $264.50. Preserve the target as an experiment; consider separately quoted delivery, a supported kit option, validated simpler geometry or pausing XL. Do not transfer large-dog loads to an inadequately tested plastic connector to preserve margin.

Budget both gross margin and contribution. Gross margin alone hides fit support and delivery, which are central to the offer. Customer-acquisition cost must include content, contractor and partner-program spend allocated to customers, not just ad clicks. Since repeat purchasing is unknown, use first-order contribution to set an acquisition ceiling.

These allocations are placeholders until quotes arrive. No funds were spent, printers purchased or services booked by this task. Keep founder living costs, taxes, financing and legal entity expenses visible as additional needs where applicable. A $2,000 contingency may be inadequate if testing reveals a major redesign.

Separate validation capital from working capital. Pay suppliers and production labor before settlement and returns close. Maintain a cash reserve for replacements and refunds. For each planned batch, estimate deposits, component lead times, assembly capacity, processor settlement, stock holding and expected return liability. Do not fund testing by accepting nonrefundable orders from distressed caregivers.

The model's break-even count divides monthly fixed cost by weighted per-kit contribution. It is not a promise that a batch recovers all startup capital. A low-volume hardware business can be viable as a small operation while failing to support full-time salaries; include market-rate labor when judging scale.

## 9. Production and supply chain

Create a versioned bill of materials, drawing set, print specification and assembly traveler for each released family. Record exact material supplier/grade, lot, drying history, nozzle, machine, slicer profile, orientation, finished mass, inspection and torque checks. A filament color change or substitute wheel can change behavior and requires controlled review.

Standardize tube diameters, fasteners and wheel interfaces across families when testing supports it. Avoid unnecessary proprietary fasteners. Use metal load paths and positive retention where practical; do not assume friction clamps or heat-set inserts alone provide a safe interface. Qualify two suppliers for critical purchased parts before promising availability.

Track printer hours, failed prints, rework, assembly and support separately. Initial production can use one qualified internal process or a tightly controlled local manufacturing partner. An open network of uncontrolled home printers is not an initial safety strategy. Release every kit only after its traveler is complete and a second-person inspection has passed.

## 10. Sales, fitting and aftercare

The proposed funnel is education, professional assessment, complete measurements, configuration review, transparent quote, payment, assembly/inspection, delivery and supervised fitting. Medical suitability remains with a qualified professional. A size calculator narrows possible geometry and never diagnoses or approves use.

Before taking payments, implement a real fitting intake, privacy/consent, order status, appropriate payment provider, taxes, delivery quotes, refund policy, support contact and webhook-driven reconciliation using native Stacks commerce/payments. No live processor is configured here. The current checkout intentionally collects no personal or payment details.

After fitting, ask about rubbing, skin pressure, alignment, wheel clearance, fastener movement and practical routine. Define who may authorize continued use after a concern. Maintain a stop-use response and an incident record. Publish replacement instructions only for operations the caregiver can safely perform. Return-window and warranty terms need operational and legal review before publication.

## 11. Launch sequence

Weeks are planning windows, conditional on evidence. They are not a shipping calendar.

| Window | Owner role | Work | Exit evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Immediate | Founder and treating vet | Buddy's care, diagnosis and available support options | Care plan, no delay for development |
| Weeks 1-2 | Founder, designer, rehab advisor | Interviews, measurements, requirements, BOM quotes | Selected first family and measurable requirements |
| Weeks 3-5 | Mechanical designer and manufacturing lead | Bench-only prototypes, coupon tests and tolerance studies | Stable drawings and qualified process hypothesis |
| Weeks 6-8 | Independent test engineer | Structural, cyclic, environmental and retention testing | Reviewed results meeting predeclared limits |
| Weeks 9-12 | Rehab advisor and pilot coordinator | Consented supervised fit pilot after bench approval | Fit/skin/handling findings and corrective actions |
| Weeks 13-16 | Founder and operations lead | Controlled first-family launch readiness | Product, claims, support and commerce gates complete |
| Later | Same accountable roles | Qualify remaining families independently | Separate evidence for every family beyond the selected first release |

Chris is the proposed accountable founder. Other roles are needed, not people already hired. Dates move when evidence is incomplete. A failed test produces redesign and retest, not a relabeled launch.

## 12. Risks and controls

The highest risks are an unsuitable aid delaying medical care, structural failure, pressure injuries, tipping, entrapment, unclear fitting, unsupported performance claims and poor unit economics. The product validation document contains the detailed register and required evidence. Zero tolerance for critical unmitigated hazards is a release gate; it is not a claim that any product is risk-free.

Commercial risks include low referral demand, high support cost, expensive freight, returns, supplier inconsistency, copying protected designs, and confusing generated imagery with proof. Address each with evidence: interviews, quotes, support logs, alternate suppliers, independent review and explicit concept labels. Do not invent customer testimonials or professional endorsements.

Software risks include accepting orders while products are unvalidated, stale prices, incorrect money units and cart access between visitors. The app uses native signed cart cookies, server-side product lookup and an unconditional order gate. Tests should cover these boundaries and the published acceptance record should disclose untested conditions.

## 13. Success metrics and open decisions

Before launch, measure suitable-case interviews, repeatable fit measurements, passing bench reports, assembly/inspection time, production yield, completed support procedures and the number of unresolved critical risks. Commercial success is a safely fitted customer with positive contribution and supported aftercare, not an email signup count.

After launch, use closed cohorts for fit acceptance, returns, warranty claims and referral conversion. Treat customers still inside an observation window as pending. Track contribution, cash, time to fitting, first-response time, incident severity, repeat repairs and part availability. Publish product evidence only with consent and an accurate denominator.

Open decisions: Buddy's clinical needs and measurements; first service region; first qualified family; mechanical/test advisors; validated material/process; final kit contents; supplier quotes; suspension feasibility and cost; real startup budget; insurance; brand clearance; support staffing; return/warranty policy; digital-file license; sales-tax setup. The founder selected buddywheels.org and the existing Stacks host; deployment evidence is tracked in the acceptance record. Other open decisions are not silently treated as approved.

The next business commitment should be the smallest evidence-producing step: secure Buddy's care, choose a qualified design/fitting advisor, and obtain real requirements and quotes for one size family.

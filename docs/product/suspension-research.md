# Adjustable suspension and rehabilitation support research

Founder-requested research track, November 7, 2026. No suspension module or rehabilitation mode is implemented, qualified or available. This is a design investigation, not a use prescription for Buddy.

## The question

Could a chair accommodate active outdoor use and provide adjustable support as an eligible dog progresses through a professionally managed rehabilitation plan? It is worth investigating, but those goals require distinct controls and evidence. A “soft to stiff” dial must not imply that it prescribes training load.

The [Cornell veterinary rehabilitation guidance](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-topics/treadmill-training-dogs) discusses body-weight support and professional assessment for compromised patients. A [clinical comparison of supported treadmill and over-ground training](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/veterinary-science/articles/10.3389/fvets.2021.597949/full) studies selected dogs with incomplete spinal cord injury under a rehabilitation protocol. These sources support investigating controlled assistance. Neither validates this wheelchair, outdoor suspension, a universal training schedule or settings for Buddy.

## Separate three engineering controls

| Control | Mechanical purpose | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spring stiffness and geometry | Determine how force changes with suspension movement | A prescribed percentage of body weight carried by the hind limbs |
| Preload and supported position | Set initial spring force/sag and operating position | A spring's stiffness or a safe rehabilitation dose by themselves |
| Damping | Control the rate of movement and reduce uncontrolled rebound | Correct fit, adequate structural strength or medical suitability |

The mass supported at the rear is not simply the dog's total body weight divided by two. Posture, harness position, front-limb loading, wheel geometry and movement all affect it. No force, stiffness, preload, travel or exercise setting is specified in this plan.

## Track A: isolate ground disturbances

Investigate short-travel wheel suspension using captured, commercially rated springs/dampers and a mechanical structure appropriate to each family. Compare it with a fixed reference frame using ballast first. Keep harness support predictable as wheels move. Independent left/right wheel travel may introduce body roll; linked or constrained alternatives need comparison. Tire compliance may prove sufficient or simpler than an added suspension.

Required concepts include a stable neutral position, captured joints, positive travel stops, a verifiable lockout, safe behavior if an adjuster moves or a damper loses function, and retained wheel/axle connections. Do not use a printed spring, friction-only adjuster or unqualified printed shock mount as a primary safety component. A component supplier rating is not a system-level wheelchair rating.

A suspension must not become an untested sit-down/collapse mechanism. The existing first-release exclusion of collapsing support remains. Extreme settings must not permit abrupt pelvic drop, toe dragging, pinch points, harness bottoming or unacceptable center-of-gravity changes.

## Track B: controlled body support

Explore a separately adjustable harness-support mechanism that can maintain professionally selected posture and unload the dog predictably. A possible interface is an indexed, lockable mechanical adjustment with a documented supported-position range. The hardware and procedure must permit measurement of actual support forces rather than treating dial position as a percentage.

Any rehabilitation-oriented adjustment would need a professional protocol, calibration, clearly identified settings, adequate range for the specific dog, safeguards against caregiver misinterpretation, and reassessment as condition changes. Do not let a softer spring automatically reduce assistance or call a stiffer setting “strength training.” Do not automatically change settings with speed, terrain or time in an initial design.

A stable default and a separate professional adjustment path are preferable research starting points to one consumer dial controlling wheel motion, harness height and unloading simultaneously. This is a hypothesis to test, not a released configuration.

## Bench comparison plan

Use a fixed frame as the baseline and compare candidate systems under the same supported load and geometry. Establish limits before measuring outcomes.

- Ground inputs: repeatable pavement/grass/firm-trail/light-gravel rigs, thresholds, slopes and one-wheel obstacles within the proposed operating envelope.
- Motion/contact: rail and supported-position acceleration, pitch/roll, pelvis-height excursion, toe clearance, harness forces and contact pressure, including asymmetry.
- Mechanical reliability: travel-stop loads, lockout retention, adjuster loosening, corrosion, dirt intrusion, cleaning, temperature, repeated loading and damper/spring failure modes.
- Serviceability: mass, snagging, caregiver setup mistakes, inspection, compatibility, replacement and parts availability.

The engineer should choose instrumentation, sample size and test loads. Use ballast, not a dog, for structural and failure exploration. A visually smoother ride is not sufficient evidence of reduced tissue load or improved rehabilitation.

## Supervised evaluation gate

Only after engineering review and bench approval should a veterinary rehabilitation professional consider a consented comparative fitting protocol. Evaluate comfort, posture, effort, pressure/skin outcomes, foot placement, stability and practical handling for the intended population. Establish stop rules and follow-up before recruitment. No clinical efficacy claim follows merely from successful mechanical tests.

Buddy's diagnosis, exact weight, measurements and permitted activity remain unknown. He must not become a first uncontrolled suspension test. His immediate care and access to appropriate available equipment proceed independently of this research.

## Commercial decision

Do not silently fold an uncosted suspension into the current half-price kit promise. Measure the actual component, assembly, testing, support, mass and service costs. Compare a fixed frame, tire-based compliance, wheel suspension and controlled-support options. Choose the simplest architecture that meets validated requirements.

The research may produce an optional qualified module, a separately evaluated advanced family, or a decision to keep the baseline fixed. Compatibility, user instructions and price must be explicit. “All-terrain” requires defined surface and operating limits; “rehabilitation support” requires a separate appropriate evidence and professional-use boundary.

## Acceptance criteria before claiming availability

- An independent engineer has signed off the system's structure, travel, stability, retention and failure behavior for the offered family.
- Settings, supported position, loads and allowed combinations are calibrated and documented.
- Fit and contact outcomes have passed a professionally designed evaluation.
- Caregivers can identify the intended setting and inspect/lock it without a critical setup error.
- Manufacturing, repair, cost, instructions and claims match the tested configuration.
- The ecommerce catalog describes a real released module; current images and prices are updated accordingly.

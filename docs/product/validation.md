# Validation, quality and release gates

Development plan, November 7, 2026. The activities below have not been performed. An independent mechanical engineer and qualified veterinary rehabilitation professional must set defensible acceptance limits before testing. This document is not a certification or a test report.

## Gate sequence

1. Requirements and hazard review: define intended user, family, supported postures, loads, adjustment limits and excluded uses. Establish who signs off each requirement.
2. Materials/process qualification: coupons and representative joints across orientation, lots, environment and known printer variability.
3. Bench system verification: ballast-only assemblies, worst-case adjustments and failure investigation.
4. Professional fitting review: instructions, contact geometry, suitability/exclusions and handling evaluated without using an unverified assembly on a dog.
5. Supervised pilot: written protocol, owner consent, veterinarian clearance, predefined stop criteria and reviewed bench evidence before animal use.
6. Commercial readiness: complete records, claims, insurance, support, replacements, returns and commerce operations. Only then consider enabling orders.

A calendar date cannot substitute for any gate. The founder is accountable for obtaining the named professional roles; those roles are not currently staffed by this project.

## Test matrix

| Requirement | Proposed verification | Acceptance definition needed before test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carry intended support loads | Static proof and ultimate tests at worst adjustment, with ballast | Supported load model, proof factor, allowable deformation and failure margin |
| Survive repeated service | Cyclic frame/joint/axle loading representative of a stated duty cycle | Cycle count, load spectrum, sample number and inspection limits |
| Retain adjustments | Pull/slip/rotation tests after torque, aging and contamination | Retention force, allowable movement and positive-lock behavior |
| Resist tip/rollover | Lateral/longitudinal stability rig across declared slopes and obstacles | Declared safe-use envelope and minimum tipping margin |
| Handle real contact events | Threshold/impact/obstacle and wheel pull-off tests | Impact energies, retention and post-impact inspection limits |
| Survive environment | Heat, moisture, UV, dirt and approved cleaning exposure | Conditioning duration, property retention and inspection rejection rules |
| Protect skin/contact | Professional review followed by approved supervised fit observations | Contact/pressure method, skin/comfort criteria and follow-up window |
| Avoid entanglement | Harness, toe, fur and pinch-path inspection across adjustments | Required clearances and unacceptable accessible hazards |
| Support correct assembly | Observed assembly by representative caregivers | Critical-error rate, time distribution, comprehension and corrective measures |
| Permit safe servicing | Repair trials using released instructions and parts | Allowed tasks, inspection, time and successful retention verification |

Do not invent a standard number or use a human-wheelchair standard as proof of animal-device compliance. The engineer should identify applicable methods and explain any adaptations. Establish confidence and sample sizes appropriate to variation; a single visually intact prototype is insufficient. Include dimensional extremes, permitted combinations, material lots and foreseeable misuse.

## Preliminary risk register

| Hazard | Potential consequence | Proposed control | Required evidence / owner role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| New paralysis treated as shopping problem | Delayed veterinary treatment | Urgent-care message and exclusion from autonomous fitting | Copy review / rehab advisor |
| Print delamination, creep or brittle fracture | Collapse or sharp fragments | Qualified process, metal retention, inspection and retirement limits | Coupon/joint/system results / mechanical engineer |
| Wrong fit or unsuitable support type | Pain, rubbing or worsened handling | Professional suitability and full fit review, adjustment limits | Supervised protocol results / rehab advisor |
| Tip or wheel catch | Fall, strain or panic | Stable geometry, declared terrain envelope and supervision | Stability/obstacle tests / mechanical engineer |
| Loose axle/fastener or slipping rail | Loss of support | Positive retention, verified assembly and routine inspection | Pull-off, fatigue and assembly tests / quality lead |
| Harness pressure or toileting interference | Skin injury, discomfort or contamination | Padded routing, individual assessment, washable supports | Contact and cleaning review / rehab advisor |
| Chewing, snagging and small parts | Ingestion or entrapment | Captive parts where appropriate, covers and inspection | Hazard/retention review / quality lead |
| Incompatible home print or repair | Unknown strength or assembly | Versioned parts/process, maker qualification and withdrawal notices | Process audit / manufacturing lead |
| Unsupported marketing claims | Misleading purchase/use | Evidence ledger and claim sign-off | Claims review / founder and advisor |
| Product sold before readiness | Unvalidated animal use | Server-side checkout gate and zero payment collection | Endpoint tests / software owner |
| No incident traceability | Delayed corrective action | Serial/lot records and published stop-use contact | Drill and record completeness / operations lead |

Severity, probability and detectability ratings require the actual design and evidence. They are intentionally not fabricated here. Record initial and residual risk with the responsible reviewer and date before approving the product. Any unresolved critical hazard blocks release.

## Supervised pilot proposal

Recruit only after bench approval. Establish eligibility, exclusions, permitted activity, supervised fitting, baseline observations, follow-up, documentation, owner consent and withdrawal without penalty. A proposed 10-20-dog fit study is an operational starting point to discuss with advisors, not statistically established safety proof. Include body-shape diversity relevant to the selected family and avoid extrapolating to unrepresented dogs.

Stop use for any structural defect, loss of retention, new pain, skin injury, instability, distress or professional concern. Provide an immediate contact and escalation path. Keep medical care decisions with the treating professional. Do not prescribe a universal session length or rehabilitation routine through the website.

## Change control and incident response

A failed test records specimen, configuration, process, condition, load history, failure mode and photographs. Review root cause, change design/process, update the revision and retest affected requirements. Do not discard failed specimens and keep only favorable results.

After release, acknowledge concerns promptly, give a clear stop-use instruction when indicated, identify the serial/lot and review whether more units are affected. Quarantine unshipped related stock while the responsible team assesses it. Define legal/reporting responsibilities with qualified advisors for each market. Retain the ability to contact affected purchasers and withdraw parts/files. “Open source” does not remove manufacturing responsibility.

## Order-enablement criteria

- Each offered family has a signed requirement and verification record.
- CAD, BOM, approved materials, process, assembly and inspection records agree.
- Professional suitability and fitting procedures, contraindications and user instructions are ready.
- Support, spare parts, return/warranty terms, incident handling and insurance are operational.
- Marketing claims and imagery accurately match the released unit.
- Payment provider, taxes, delivery, inventory, refunds and webhook reconciliation pass integration tests.
- The application checkout gate is changed through a reviewed release with the evidence record attached.

None of these physical-product gates is reported complete by the website implementation.

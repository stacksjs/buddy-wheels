# Rover r0 scope, native geometry and verification

November 11, 2026. This records what the first Rover design package is, how its files are made and what was actually checked. It is a bench design. No part has been printed, cut, sewn, assembled, loaded or used with an animal.

## Request and scope

The owner asked for printable Rover designs, a cabin much larger than the Burley Bark Ranger, an entrance with a minimal incline because Buddy can no longer step high, complete materials including the cloth for rain, wind and sun, updated documents and a working deployment.

Delivered in this revision:

- A parametric frame, ramp, running-gear layout and cover, in `app/Designs/Rover*.ts`.
- Eleven printable service and fixture types and six fit-check copies of the metal profiles, as STL. Six metal profiles as DXF.
- A dimensioned side and top drawing, cut list, parts list, joint pattern table and build order.
- A weather system with a named, priced candidate for every layer and a cover cut schedule.
- A cost study from the counted parts list.
- An interactive studio at `/design-studio-rover` and a configured download at `/api/designs/rover/{artifact}`.

Not in this revision: a selected wheel, a brake design, a swivel front wheel, a folding mechanism, beach or ski conversions, sewing patterns beyond rectangular blanks, any structural calculation and any physical test.

## Design decisions

| Decision | Reason | Open risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tailgate is the ramp, top-hinged leaves, 6 mm gap | One part does two jobs, and the dog walks on a continuous surface from ground to deck | Hinge pinch point, latch and strap retention |
| Ramp stands on its own two feet | A 140 lb dog on the threshold would otherwise tip the carrier backward about its rear axle | Leg and gusset strength, foot setting on uneven ground |
| Deck fixed at 151.9 mm for every wheel choice | The base rail stays 124 mm up; each wheel has a hanger profile with the axle at its own fixed rise, 41, 69 or 130 mm | Only 108 mm ground clearance. Coast and Snow may need more |
| Incline limit 12 degrees, start about 9 | The owner asked for a minimal incline. Published ramp guidance for dogs varies, so a strict limit was chosen rather than a cited one | Not validated with any dog. Wet grip unproven |
| Three matching wheels, 15 inch 4.00-8 trail wheel by default | One wheel, one hanger, one spare. The supplier states bearings, bore, hub length and load, so nothing about the axle is guessed | A utility wheel, not a running wheel. Its weight is not stated. The 13 inch option is cheaper and the 20 inch option has no verified candidate |
| One carrier, built two ways: bent and welded by a shop as the starting build, bolted at home as the option | The shop build has 10 fewer tubes, 52 fewer plates and about 160 fewer bolts, saves about 6.4 kg on paper and arrives as one frame. Its five bent tubes are round so a mail-order service can bend them. Bolting matches the XL r1 standard-parts approach and can be built with a drill | No weld procedure, sample joint or welding quote, and no bent tube has been ordered. Welding softens the tube beside each joint and nothing allows for it. The round 6063-T52 hoops are a softer and smaller section than the square tube they replace. The bolted frame has about 170 sleeved joints. The welded frame counts about 4% more; see the cost study |
| Swivel front caster with a swivel lock on a raised nose, fixed fork kept as an option | A 140 lb dog makes a lift-to-steer front wheel impractical at walking pace; the lock holds it straight for running | The priced caster states its height but not its plate or bolt pattern, so the beams are drilled from the delivered plate |
| Parking brake from a push-pull toggle clamp over each rear tire | Standard parts, positive over-center lock, no new mechanism to machine | Untested. Holding force on a pneumatic tire depends on adjustment and pressure. No brake for slowing |
| Zinc-plated grade 5 fasteners in Run, aluminum gussets | Lower cost and higher strength than 18-8 stainless | Corrosion in salt. Stainless is the Coast upgrade, with galling to manage |

## Cost-down pass, November 12, 2026

The owner asked for the price to come down. The same r0 design was revised before anything was built: no part geometry changed, so every STL and DXF is byte-identical, while quantities, tube walls, hardware, panel grade and the cover specification changed. Earlier downloads of the documents are superseded.

- Intermediate base cross members and both ramp ribs are thin-wall tube. A 700 mm cross member carrying a 450 N share at mid span works out near 68 MPa by hand arithmetic. End cross members, rails, axle posts, fork and ramp curbs stay 1/8 inch wall.
- The eight corner-post joints use one outside gusset instead of a pair. Axle posts, which take the handle and the wheels, keep pairs.
- Run hardware is zinc-plated grade 5. Panels are sealed exterior BC plywood.
- The cover keeps the solution-dyed fabric on the roof and uses a cheaper PVC-coated 600 denier polyester on the walls, drops the zippers for hook and loop, tapes only the roof seams and makes the reflective liner optional.
- Listed supplier quantity tiers are used where the 100-carrier purchase reaches them.

Result: $1,668.26 per carrier at 100 units and $2,809.28 for one, from $2,037.93 and $3,254.89. Tube and wheel prices could not be verified lower; supplier pages refused automated reads on both days. The study therefore also reports a second total with tube bought by weight at an assumed $4.00 per pound, $1,296.72, and labels it as an assumption.

A second pass the same day made the 13 x 4 inch pneumatic wheel from the XL wheelchair the default, with a low axle hanger profile and spacers so its wide tire clears the fork. Its $14.99 price was observed on November 8; the supplier page refused a recheck on November 12. That took the totals to $1,527.23 and $2,674.25, and $1,159.53 with tube by weight. The 20 inch wheel remains a studio option at its $60 allowance. This pass added one metal profile; the other fourteen meshes and three profiles are unchanged.

A third pass read prices in a real browser on supplier pages that refuse automated reads. It moved the tube to 21 ft and 24 ft stock bars from Metals Depot, with the cabin and secondary members in 1/16 inch 6063-T52 and the chassis in 1/8 inch 6061-T6; made a 15 inch pneumatic wheel with stated bearings, hub length and load the default; and replaced the bolt, nut, washer and plywood allowances with observed prices. Totals are $1,365.53 at 100 units, $2,589.59 for one and $1,186.31 with tube by weight. The thin wall changed from .065 to 1/16 inch, so the cabin tube cap mesh changed; one more hanger profile was added. A one-carrier buy list is generated with the kit.

Looked at and not adopted: a 10 inch swivel caster for the front wheel, which would cut steering effort but stands above the deck and needs a raised front frame; two wheelchair wheel-lock sets for the brake, both made for specific chairs; tent-style hoops instead of the box cabin, which would narrow the headroom that makes the cabin larger than the reference.

A fourth pass designed the swivel front. A 10 inch caster stands about 311 mm tall, above the 152 mm deck, so it cannot go under the floor. The base rails are extended about 330 mm and carry four nose posts and two caster beams, all joined with the existing corner gusset profile; the caster bolts up under the beams with its wheel sweep clear of the cabin front. The caster mounting height is a setup input because the priced caster's supplier does not state it. This replaces the fork, front axle, third wheel and four hangers, for a net of about $19 per carrier.

The same pass added what a builder needs to make one: a build guide in order of work, a plan of the base frame from above, a drilling schedule for every tube measured from a named end, a nesting plan into 8 ft parcel bars, and a buy list. One-carrier costs now assume 8 ft bars, the longest the tube supplier ships by parcel.

Parking is not solved. Cart floor locks were read at $42 to $100; the one that would fit under the floor hangs about 11 mm above the ground when retracted. Wheelchair wheel locks are made for specific chairs. The design specifies wheel chocks for bench trials and leaves a wheel brake open.

A fifth pass closed the open items that could be closed on paper. The front caster is now a Service Caster model with a bolt-on swivel lock and a stated 12-3/16 inch mounting height, read at $64.69. The parking brake is a POWERTEC 302F push-pull toggle clamp, read at $10.70 with a stated 300 lb holding capacity, hung on a new brake clamp plate from a short arm bolted to each axle post with the existing corner gusset; a rubber bumper on its plunger presses the tread. Hinge, latches, leveling mounts, sleeve tube, axle tube, bolts, washers, cotters and chocks are priced from supplier pages, and the buy list for one build is now about 84% verified by value. Totals are $1,507.81 at 100 units, $2,777.43 for one and $1,309.43 with tube by weight.

A sixth pass closed three fits. The maker's shop page for the caster series lists a 4 x 4-1/2 inch plate with holes at 2-5/8 x 3-5/8 inch slotted to 3 x 3 inch, so the caster beam holes are now drawn 85.7 by 70 mm inside those slots instead of being left to match-drilling. The brake pad is a DE-STA-CO neoprene-tipped spindle whose 5/16-18 thread is stated and matches the clamp plunger, read at $4.15. No threaded insert for 1 inch square tube with a 1/8 inch wall could be found, so the ramp stand legs are now a fixed cut length ending in a printed chassis cap, with no foot and no insert. Totals are $1,481.08 at 100 units, $2,749.54 for one and $1,281.97 with tube by weight.

On the same day the owner set the Run price to $999 and asked for a slightly larger cabin. The starting size is now 1300 x 700 x 1000 mm clear with a 1000 mm ramp at about 8 degrees; the width stays 700 because the carrier is already about 1008 mm wide over its axle ends. Totals at that size are $1,538.35 at 100 units, $2,792.64 for one and $1,334.08 with tube by weight, all above the $999 price. The cost study now carries a section on how comparable products reach their prices and what each lever would do here.

The owner also asked for the smoothest ride available and for a conversion to carry two children. Both were taken up in a later pass, described below, as far as paper allows: a drawn rear suspension with no ride claim, and a two-seat layout concept that is not a child carrier.

A further pass answered two requests about shape and options. The front wall is raked: the front posts and top frame stand 350 mm back and the wall is fabric on two 12.7 mm stays in sewn sleeves, so nothing is bent and no new plate is needed. Cross members are re-spaced automatically to keep their bolts clear of the moved posts. Four activity setups were added to the studio. Walk, Run and Coast are combinations of options that already existed. Snow adds a conversion kit drawn from the same tube and tee gussets: a post that swings on each rear axle, a tube foot bolted to a purchased ski, and two tow shafts pinned to the front. No ski, pin or belt has been selected or priced, the skier carries the front of the carrier, and there is no brake on snow. Totals for the starting Walk setup are $1,518.19 at 100 units, $2,769.85 for one and $1,318.14 with tube by weight; the Snow setup adds about $213 on allowances.

A welded pass followed at the owner's request. The frame gained a build setting. Welded is now the starting design: the top rails fall 180 mm from rear to front, a raked pillar on each side replaces the front post and the fabric stay, and every gusset is gone. A weld schedule lists the 46 joints, the cut list carries the angled ends, the drilling schedule shrinks to the hangers, brake plates, caster, tip-stops and ramp, and the build guide becomes a guide to briefing a fabrication shop and checking the frame it returns. The bolted frame is unchanged and still selectable; its roof cannot taper. The cost model gained a welding line on assumed shop rates: $202.84 a carrier at 100 units and $690 for one. Totals for the welded starting setup are $1,504.41 at 100 units, $2,970.43 for one and $1,308.97 with tube by weight, against $1,518.19 and $2,769.85 bolted. Welding therefore does not lower the cost; it lowers the mass estimate from 51.3 to 43.6 kg and gives the shape. A welded frame ships assembled, and that freight is not counted.

A ride and seating pass followed. The rear wheels moved from hangers on the axle posts to trailing swing arms outside the base rails. Each arm pivots on one 1/2 inch bolt through the rail, 300 mm ahead of the axle, and carries a 150 mm coil-over shock between welded tabs on the arm and tabs under a stub on the axle post. A new shock tab profile was added, which makes seven metal profiles and eighteen meshes. The arithmetic gives a 0.51 motion ratio, a 20.7 N/mm rate at the wheel, about 59 mm of travel and about 22 mm of settle under the planning mass, using a candidate shock read at $31.05 whose spring is labeled 450 lbs by its seller. That label is not a verified rate, no shock has been fitted and no ride has been measured, so nothing is claimed against any other product. The sprung rear needs the welded frame and is not drawn for the 20 inch wheel, where the arm is too steep. It adds $120.31 at 100 units and widens the carrier to about 1080 mm. Totals for the starting setup are now $1,624.72 at 100 units, $3,251.09 for one and $1,422.55 with tube by weight. Open: a single-bolt pivot with a 25 mm bearing spread, the parking brake pad on a tire that moves with the load, and the unsprung caster.

The same pass added a two-seat bench as a studio option: three cross tubes, two legs, a seat board and a back sling just ahead of the axle. It is a layout concept labeled not for use with a child. Its joints, anchors and restraints are deliberately not designed and no build steps are issued, because a carrier for children is a regulated children's product (16 CFR part 1227 and ASTM F833-21 for a pushed one, third-party testing and a Children's Product Certificate). It is priced as a $90 allowance.

A fit error in the earlier parts list was also found: the linked 3/4 inch x .065 wall axle sleeve tube is 0.620 inch inside, 0.005 under the 5/8 inch axle. The guide and the allowance notes now say to ream it or buy .058 wall.

A home-build pass followed, after the owner asked whether the whole carrier could be made at home and whether it could copy the reference maker's frame. A read of that maker's pages and manual found a folding frame of several separate tubes that pivot and latch, described only as heat-treated aluminum; no page says it is one piece or how the tubes are joined, and the 6061-T6 figure comes from a third-party review. No mail-order service was found that bends square tube: the one bending service with instant pricing bends round 6063-T52 tube only. So the frame stays straight square tube, and the bolted build was brought level with the welded one. Both now share every tube: raked pillars, a roof that falls toward the front and the sprung rear. Three bolted joints are not square, so their plates are generated for each setup at the angle it needs, with the rail holes shifted to keep 15 mm from crossing bolts. The suspension mounts became bolt-on plates for both builds: a pair on the swing arm and a pair hung from the axle post around a spacer tube. The earlier fabric windshield stays are gone. The bolted build is now the starting setup, and its handle comes down to 930 mm because the roof plates sit below the roof on the axle post. Totals for the starting setup are $1,607.79 at 100 units and $2,932.51 for one; welded, $1,591.46 and $3,105.09. One laser service lists tube cutting for both of the tubes used here and cuts from one 3D file per part.

A tube file pass followed. The bolted kit now carries a STEP file for every different frame tube, 20 files for 39 pieces at the starting size, each modeled as the plain square tube with its holes cut through single walls and its angled ends, plus an order sheet with the quantity and material for each. Hole positions are read from the drilling schedule, so the two cannot disagree. The files come from a small native boundary-representation kit and STEP writer. Each solid is checked for paired edges, closed loops, vertices on their surfaces, the expected number of handles and a volume that matches section area times length less the holes. Three kinds of hole are left for hand work and listed: the 19.25 mm axle sleeve bore, which is nearly as wide as the tube, the caster plate holes, which are marked from the delivered caster, and any end steeper than 44 degrees, which is left square for the saw. The thin-wall tube is modeled at the 0.062 inch the service lists against the 1/16 inch priced from the bar supplier.

The files were then tried for real. With the owner's permission all 20 were uploaded to OSH Cut's instant-quote page on November 12, 2026. Every file was recognized as a tube and passed the design check, and the page matched each to the intended material on its own. The set of 39 tubes was quoted at $1,405.06 before shipping and tax, shipping in 8 business days; bars for the same tubes cost $639.68, so the laser route costs about $765 more and removes 39 saw cuts and 451 drilled tube walls. Nothing was ordered. Three changes followed from doing the order by hand. The floor fastener holes, which had no positions and were drilled on assembly, are now placed in the drilling schedule and cut by the laser as one-wall holes clear of every post, bolt and sleeve. The one bore the laser cannot cut gets a 5 mm pilot hole so nothing has to be measured. And the files are sorted into one folder per material with the quantity in each name, because the service only sets a material on parts of one profile at a time and does not read quantities. Ready-cut bolt sleeves were also found, at $184.32 for 250, against 190 saw cuts. Not done: the files were not opened in a separate CAD program, no size other than the starting one was uploaded, and no tube has been cut.

A cost pass followed, on the owner's instruction to find real reductions. The eight plate files were added to the same quote. All passed, and the plates came to about double the allowance carried until then: $9.47 for a corner gusset and $8.19 for a tee in 6061-T6, $551.22 for one carrier's 62 plates. The one-carrier cost model now uses those prices, which raised its total; the 100-carrier plate figure is still an unquoted allowance and is probably low. Three reductions were tested with quotes. The same plates in 5052-H32 came to $383.84, which is offered as a choice and not adopted, because it is the softer alloy and nothing has been calculated in either. The tubes were packed end to end into 8 sticks with marker holes at 25 saw lines, on the theory that the service charged mostly per piece. It does not: the set fell only from $1,405.06 to $1,195.44, because most of the price is the service's rate for the metal, about twice the bar price. The sticks replace the single-tube files in the kit anyway, as the cheaper of the two laser routes, but bars cut and drilled by hand remain far cheaper. One joint was simplified: the handle grip now lies across the tops of the arm ends on two bolts in place of four gussets and eight bolts. The whole metal order, sticks and plates, was quoted at $1,746.66. The owner then made two decisions. The plates stay in 6061-T6: a softer alloy would save $167 a carrier, but the plates hold every joint and nothing has been calculated. And the tube for one carrier is bought as full 21 ft and 24 ft bars, each cut in half by the supplier to carry, which is now the counted purchase: $429.51 at one online supplier's listed long-bar prices against $639.68 for the same tube as 8 ft parcel bars. A local supplier's counter price was not checked. Materials for one bolted build come to about $2,063, and the laser sticks cost about $766 more than those bars.

A bent-frame pass followed, after the owner asked why the frame still had so many tubes and asked for it to arrive mostly assembled. The welded frame became the starting setup again and three groups of its tubes became single bent parts. Each cabin side is one hoop: rear post, roof run and front pillar in one tube with two bends. The handle is one U-shaped tube lapped onto the roof runs. Each end of the caster nose is one small hoop in place of two posts and a beam. That is 29 tubes, 10 bends and 36 welded joints at the starting size, down from 39 tubes and 46 joints. Every bend uses one centerline radius, 4.5 inches, the tightest with a published clean result for 1 inch square aluminum tube with a 1/8 inch wall; a 3 inch radius was drawn first and dropped for lack of evidence, and the ramp legs stay straight. A bend schedule gives each part as straight runs and bend angles with its developed length, the cut list carries that length, and the scene draws the bends, so the scene's tube total still equals the cut list. A setup whose bends would leave no straight between them is refused. Bending is a counted cost line beside welding, both arithmetic on assumed shop rates.

A search for someone to make it found no mail-order service that bends square tube. Xometry and OSH Cut bend round tube; SendCutSend and Fabworks do not bend tube. This was checked first-hand in OSH Cut's bent tube tool on November 12, 2026: with its bendable filter on, the catalog lists round tube only, in steel, stainless and 6063-T52 aluminum. The bend schedule now gives each tube as the corner points the service's order form asks for, in inches, beside the runs and angles. The side hoop is ordered 5.3 mm long at its front foot, which is sawn to the pillar's lean after bending. The scene draws the bent tubes round. With the owner's permission the three parts were entered in OSH Cut's bent tube tool on November 12, 2026. All passed the design check and were quoted at $100.50 a side hoop, $116.09 for the handle bar and $61.65 a nose hoop, $440.39 for one carrier, and at $61.45, $58.04 and $28.43 at the quantities for 100 carriers. Those quotes replace the bending allowance and the bar stock for those tubes in the cost model, which raised the welded totals to $1,666.02 at 100 units and $3,082.31 for one: the earlier bending figure was arithmetic and was low. The estimated mass rose to about 45.9 kg because the round tube has a 1/8 inch wall. With the bolted kit taken out of that cart, the welded frame's ten plates were quoted as an order of their own at $71.00 ($13.66 a brake plate, $5.51 a shock plate, $5.41 a shock hanger) against $60.06 when they shared the larger order, and the one-carrier cost model uses the higher figure, which makes that total $3,093.25. The whole cart, five bent tubes and ten plates, was $511.39 at a standard lead of 6 business days.

On November 12, 2026 the owner had a welding quote request emailed to the four of those shops that publish an email address: Level 7 Fabrication, B & E Enterprises, Tru-Form and Precision Tube Bending. Each got the two dimensioned drawings and the quote pack, and was asked for a price and lead time to weld the frame with the bent tubes supplied, or to quote the bending as well. N8Z Fab and One-Way Manufacturing list only a phone number and have not been contacted. What the round tubes leave open. The nose hoops carry the caster and were 1/8 inch wall 6061-T6 square tube; the round tube has about 59% of that section's bending stiffness and is the softer alloy, and nothing has been calculated for either. The handle bar arms are welded where a round tube crosses a round hoop leg, which is a small weld; the shop is asked whether to add a through bolt or a gusset. The caster plate bolts up under two round tubes on line contact. The printed cover clips and the ramp latch keepers were drawn for square tube and are untried on round. The service offers 1.25 and 1.5 inch round tube in the same alloy and wall, which is the first thing to try if the nose or the handle needs more section.

Not closed: no fabricator has seen the drawings, no weld sample exists, and no bent tube has been ordered. The caster's swivel radius and the spindle's tip diameter are unconfirmed. Nothing has been built or loaded.

What these passes could not do is make the design competitive with the reference trailer's price. The bent hoops are a step toward the architecture such products use and cut the tube and joint count, but at a prototype service's prices they raise the counted cost by a few percent. Closing the gap still needs a lighter fabric cabin, thinner round tube bent on production tooling, and factory quotes rather than listings.

## Native geometry

OpenSCAD is not installed on the design workstation any more, and running a freshly downloaded copy was not approved in this session. Rover geometry is therefore generated by a small native kit, `app/Designs/Solid.ts`: stacked extrusions of 2D outlines with holes, ear-clipping triangulation, and one rigid rotation into print orientation. It needs no external program and runs in the test suite.

The kit refuses outlines that touch or cross between neighbouring layers, which is the condition that makes its output a closed single body. Each exported mesh is then read back as a binary STL by the same inspector the wheelchair exports use and must be closed, consistently wound, one connected body, positive in volume, on the bed, and inside 220 x 220 x 100 mm. The flat metal fit-check copies must match their profile area times plate thickness.

There is no SCAD or STEP file for Rover. The TypeScript generator is the editable source. Wheelchair designs still use their existing OpenSCAD sources.

## What was verified

- Nine native tests cover the tube solids and their STEP files, the sprung rear geometry and its ride arithmetic, the seat concept, the solid kit, setup validation across the range corners, all fourteen meshes, scene and cut-list agreement for both the welded and the bolted build, gusset, weld and bend counts, the bend path arithmetic, the cost arithmetic, document content and the configured kit with stale and tampered asset rejection.
- The studio page was loaded in a browser: the 3D model reached its ready state with no console errors, the ramp, side view and weather cover were inspected, and the configured downloads resolved.
- Supplier prices for the cover materials were read from supplier pages on November 11, 2026. Most hardware prices could not be read from a supplier page that day and are labeled as allowances.

## What was not verified

Everything physical. Strength, stiffness, fatigue, tip stability, braking, ramp grip, hinge and latch retention, wheel fit, cover fit, water resistance, sun protection, cabin temperature, print fit and print strength. The mass and handle-force figures are arithmetic from nominal densities and statics.
